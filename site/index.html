Packs

Teach it your world.

The core of Confidant stays deliberately simple and human — people, conversations, notes. A pack is a new shard that locks onto that core and teaches it the language of your world. And you don't write it by hand: an agent can author a pack for you. Describe how your world works in plain language, and the pack it drafts becomes a fluency Confidant — and every agent working from your memory — speaks from then on.

Authored for you

Your agent writes the pack

There's a pack-author skill for exactly this. You describe your world in plain language; your agent drafts the pack — verbs, metrics, rules — and you approve it before anything is installed. From then on it's declared in your vault, versioned like packs = ["your-world@0.1"], and confidant check verifies your memory against its rules.

One memory underneath. A new fluency on top, whenever you need it.

Why Confidant

Scattered to everyone else. Whole to you.

Most tools keep your relationships in one very readable, very breachable place. Confidant takes the opposite bet: pieces only you can assemble.

1

An encrypted vault

Contacts and notes are encrypted on your device before they're stored. Anyone who finds the files finds confetti, not a contact list.

2

Backed by git

Your vault is a git repo, so every change is versioned and syncable anywhere git goes — while the remote only ever holds sealed pieces.

3

Search that stays home

Ask for a name, a company, a half-remembered detail. Search runs locally and decrypts in memory — nothing is sent anywhere to be "processed."

4

A memory your agents share

Your AI helpers brief themselves from the same vault you keep — who's who, what you discussed, what's next. One shared memory, on your machine, owned by you.

5

No cloud, no account

No subscription seat-tax on your own friendships, no company sitting on a map of everyone you know. There's no server because there doesn't need to be.

6

Open source, in Rust

Apache-2.0 and fast. Don't take our word for any of this — the whole puzzle box is on GitHub for anyone to inspect.

How it works

Three steps to reassembled.

1

Make your vault

One command creates an encrypted, git-backed vault on your machine and mints your key locally. The pieces start out yours.

confidant init
2

Collect the pieces

Add people, organizations, and notes as life happens. Each one is sealed and committed — small fragments, safely scattered.

confidant add "…"
3

Put it together

Before a call, pull the whole picture back together in a keystroke — for you, or as a briefing for your agents.

confidant find "…"

The CLI moment

Watch the pieces click.

Privacy, by construction

Pieces only you can assemble.

Privacy here isn't a promise in a policy document. It's what falls out of how the thing is built.

Encryption happens on your device, with a key generated there and kept there. Sync moves sealed fragments through git — history and shape, never contents. There is no Confidant server holding a readable copy of your life, because there is no Confidant server.

And since the code is fully open source, "trust us" is never the ask. Check the pieces yourself.

  • Your key, your deviceMinted locally, never uploaded, never escrowed.
  • Ciphertext in transitGit remotes store sealed records — nothing readable.
  • No accounts, no trackingNothing to sign up for; nothing phones home.
  • Yours to walk away withThe vault is plain files in a folder you own.

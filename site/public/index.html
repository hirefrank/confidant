Why Confidant?

Stop introducing the same people to every agent.

One agent knows about your last coffee with Robin. Another helps with email. A third is new. Each needs an introduction before it can help well.

That repeated onboarding makes it harder to use more than one agent — or to leave one behind. Confidant keeps the relationship record in your vault. Connect a new agent and point it to the same source.

Set up each agent once. Keep the story with you.

People, not accounts

A person has a story, shared places, and a history with you. They aren't a deal in a sales pipeline.

Portable by design

The working record is plain text in your Git repo. Read it, diff it, back it up, or move it to another machine.

One source for many agents

Each agent still needs a connection and instructions. You don't have to rebuild every relationship inside its private memory.

People first

Remember the person, not just the task.

A relationship is more than a contact field. Confidant keeps how you know someone, what matters to them, and your history together in one record your connected agents can use.

How schema packs work ↗

Your boundaries

One record doesn't mean unlimited access.

You decide what your agents can read and which changes become part of the record.

Some things are just for you.

Mark a record no-ai and Confidant leaves it out of agent-facing reads. This boundary is enforced by Confidant's tools; it does not prevent an agent or person with direct file access from reading the underlying plaintext.

Your agents can propose changes for you to review. The files and Git history live in a vault you control, on a trusted, encrypted device. There is no Confidant server or account.

  • Read with boundariesno-ai keeps marked records out of agent-facing output.
  • Approve changesReview a proposed write before applying it.
  • Own the filesPlain text in your Git repo. Use a private remote if you sync.
